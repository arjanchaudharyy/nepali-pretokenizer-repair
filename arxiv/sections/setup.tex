\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

This section follows the second pre-registration amendment (Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}), which reduced the registered design to fit our budget before any continued-pretraining result was observed. Full details are in Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}.

\paragraph{Models.} Llama-3.2-1B (base) and Qwen3-0.6B-Base, two widely used open models whose tokenizers ship the letters-only regex (\S\ref{sec:audit}). Both tie input and output embeddings. Qwen3-0.6B-Base shares the tokenizer of Qwen3-1.7B-Base, on which we built the Qwen tokenizers of \S\ref{sec:compression}. The base models are evaluated as a reference.

\paragraph{Data and tokenizers.} Nepali text is FineWeb-2 \texttt{npi\_Deva}; English is FineWeb-Edu (\texttt{sample-10BT}). We normalise to NFC, deduplicate, and remove every document that shares a whitespace 10-gram with FLORES-200 dev or devtest. A 30\,MB held-out slice of each language is never trained on. \RI{} and \RII{} learn merges on the same 1\,GB of Nepali text. We sweep $K \in \{1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64\}\times 10^3$ for compression (\S\ref{sec:compression}) and use the pre-registered $K{=}32{,}000$ for training.

\paragraph{Continued pretraining.} Each arm is trained for one epoch on the \emph{same documents}: 1\,GB of Nepali and 1\,GB of English, interleaved in one fixed order. Because \RII{} encodes the same bytes in fewer tokens, it takes fewer steps and less compute. For an equal-compute view, \RO{} and \RI{} also save a checkpoint at the step count where \RII{} ends, before their cosine schedules have annealed. Optimisation is identical across arms, with a fixed, untuned peak learning rate of $10^{-4}$ and one seed (seed 0) per arm.

\paragraph{Evaluation.} Every metric is normalised by UTF-8 bytes, never by tokens. The primary metric is \emph{byte-matched} bits per byte (BPB) on held-out native Nepali: each document is split at whitespace into pieces of about 2{,}000 bytes, scored independently, so every arm conditions on the same bytes. Secondary metrics are held-out English BPB, sliding-window and FLORES-200 BPB, zero-shot Belebele~\citep{bandarkar-etal-2024-belebele}, 5-shot FLORES translation (chrF++) and Nepali generation speed in bytes per second.

\paragraph{Analysis.} The primary comparison is \RII{} against \RI{} and against \RO{} on byte-matched held-out Nepali BPB, at equal data (final checkpoints). We use a paired bootstrap over held-out pieces ($B{=}10{,}000$) of the per-piece BPB difference, and report the mean difference with its 95\% interval. We call \RII{} better if the interval of (\RII{} $-$ other) lies entirely below 0, non-inferior if it lies entirely below $+0.01$ bits per byte, and otherwise report no detectable difference. The same test on English pieces is the English control (\RII{} against \RO{}). With one seed, we cannot separate arm effects from data-order variance.
