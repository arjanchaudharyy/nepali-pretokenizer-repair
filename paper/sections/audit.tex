\section{Audit of deployed tokenizers}
\label{sec:audit}

We measured 33 released tokenizers on FLORES-200 devtest. Nine produce token ids identical to another tokenizer's on every sentence of five languages we checked (for example, Phi-4, OLMo-2 and Granite-4.1 match \texttt{cl100k}, and Qwen3 matches Qwen2.5), which leaves 24 distinct tokenizers (Figure~\ref{fig:audit}, Appendix~\ref{app:audit}). We classify each by its own pre-tokenizer configuration, since models in one family do not always share a regex. Four SentencePiece and WordPiece tokenizers (XLM-R, mT5, NLLB-200, IndicBERTv2) do not round-trip their input, so their low premiums are not comparable; we mark them and leave them out.

Eight distinct tokenizers (13 released models, including GLM-5.3 and LFM2) use the letters-only class, and none of them can be extended below $2.15$. Leaving aside GPT-2 ($7.55$), which we include only for reference, their premiums range from $2.58$ (Llama-3) to $5.77$ (LFM2). The seven lossless tokenizers with a mark-aware regex range from $1.12$ (Arkios;~\citealp{regmi2026arkios}) to $2.97$ (DeepSeek-V3).

We split each premium into three factors (Appendix~\ref{app:decomp}). The base term, $0.77$ to $0.80$ for every tokenizer, is what the premium would be if every Nepali pre-token under the repaired regex were a single token. The regex factor, $2.77$ to $2.79$ for the Llama-3, Qwen and \texttt{cl100k} pattern, counts the extra pre-tokens the tokenizer's own regex creates. The vocabulary factor, tokens per pre-token, is the only one that merge-based extension can reduce, and it cannot go below 1. For Llama-3 it is already $1.18$, so most of the remaining premium sits in the regex factor. The factors are an accounting identity. The regex factor measures room for improvement, and repairing the regex alone does not lower the premium (\S\ref{sec:compression}). Among mark-aware tokenizers the vocabulary factor still ranges from $1.43$ (Arkios) to $3.79$ (DeepSeek-V3), so a good regex also needs Nepali merges. Within families, the Nepali premium halves from Qwen3 to Qwen3.5 ($4.42$ to $2.20$), whose word branch is close to our repaired class~\citep{land2026smallbugs}, and falls from $2.58$ to $1.83$ from Llama-3 to Llama-4, which adopts \texttt{o200k}'s pattern. Both vocabularies also grew, so these changes are consistent with our account without isolating the regex.

A well-formed Devanagari syllable never starts with a combining mark, yet 55\% of Llama-3's Nepali tokens do. Even \texttt{o200k}'s mark-aware regex leaves 26\%, because BPE also splits inside words.
