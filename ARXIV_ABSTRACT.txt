Llama-3, Qwen3 and many other open LLMs pre-tokenize text with a regular expression whose word class admits only Unicode letters. Devanagari vowel signs are combining marks, so this regex splits Nepali words at every vowel sign, and current open models that use it spend 2.6 to 5.8 times as many tokens on Nepali as on the same text in English. We ask what this means for adapting a model that is already deployed. Merge-based vocabulary extension cannot join the split pieces: for Llama-3 and Qwen3 no number of new merges brings the premium below about 2.2, and ten released extensions for six Indic and Southeast Asian scripts already end within 4% of this pre-token floor. We give a small edit to the deployed regex that provably changes no token id for text without combining marks or Devanagari characters. With 32,000 new merges it brings Nepali to 1.01 times English. We then compared no extension, standard extension and the repair plus extension in continued pretraining of Llama-3.2-1B and Qwen3-0.6B on 1 GB of Nepali and 1 GB of English, against an analysis plan written in advance. Our main hypothesis failed. Continued pretraining without new tokens gave the best held-out Nepali bits per byte on both models, and the repair was worse than standard extension on Nepali by 0.026 bits per byte (about 5%) for Llama and 0.009 for Qwen, while slightly better on English. At equal bytes the repaired model also takes 26% fewer training steps, so these numbers measure the cost of one training recipe, not of the tokenizer alone. In exchange, it needs less than half as many decoding steps as standard extension for the same Nepali text. We release the tokenizers and code.
